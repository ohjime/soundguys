CoSounds/Stage four/Design memo

One vector, two certainties

The profiling roadblock isn’t two formats that need reconciling. It’s one format at two levels of confidence — and half of it is already in the schema.

A reading of src/server against the recommender literature.
What to build, in what order, and what it costs in Django.

The field notes end on a problem: a signed-up voter gives three signals, an anonymous voter gives one, and nothing downstream can be built until those resolve into a single way of describing a listener. That is the right thing to be stuck on. But reading the vote path alongside the literature, the obstacle is narrower than it looks, and the reason is sitting in login/views.py.

Finding one

You don’t have anonymous voters

You have anonymous accounts. submit_vote refuses an unauthenticated request outright and pushes the login card instead (vote/views.py:37). The “continue anonymously” path in login_anonymously mints a real User with an @anon.cosound.ca address, calls Listener.objects.get_or_create, and logs them in. Every vote in the table already points at a Listener row through a non-nullable FK.

So there is no second entity to harmonise with. Both kinds of voter are the same row in the same table. What differs is not their format — it is how much you know about them.

Not two profile shapes. One shape, held with two different amounts of confidence.

That reframing matters because “two representations that must be reconciled” is a genuinely hard modelling problem with no clean answer, whereas “one representation, sparsely observed for some users” is the cold-start problem, which has forty years of literature and a standard shape: a profile is a posterior — a mean vector plus a confidence — not a point. A survey sets the prior. Votes shrink the variance. An anonymous listener with two taps isn’t a different kind of object; they’re the same object sitting near the population mean with a wide covariance, which is exactly what you want, because it makes the system appropriately unwilling to bet on them.

Finding two

The joint space is already half-built

Sound.embeddings is a pgvector VectorField, and migration core/0001_initial already runs VectorExtension(). The classifier behind it is pluggable through COSOUND_SOUND_CLASSIFIER, with core/classify.py holding a five-dimensional random stub as the fallback. Whoever wired that up was building toward exactly this and stopped one model short.

The missing half is the listener side of the same space. That is the whole idea behind latent-factor collaborative filtering: put users and items in one shared Rd, and let a dot product mean “how much does this person want this thing.” Once a listener is a vector in the same space as a sound, votes, surveys and web activity stop being three incompatible signals. They become three different ways of getting evidence about one vector.

Anchor the first two dimensions

Here is where your own research folder pays off. A purely learned latent space is only identified up to rotation — the axes mean nothing, and nothing connects to anyone else’s paper. So don’t learn all of them. Pin dimensions 0 and 1 to the ISO 12913 circumplex, pleasantness and eventfulness, and let dimensions 2…d stay free and behavioural.

Those two anchored axes are the only thing in the system that fixes the coordinate frame, and only surveys and artists can write to them. Which turns out to be the actual answer to the harmony question: anonymous voters, who never fill in a survey, ride on the coordinate system that surveyed listeners establish. Their votes still move them around a plane whose orientation somebody else paid for. That is the join — not a merge of two formats, but a shared, externally-grounded frame that cheap signal can be projected into.

The literature

What to take, what to leave

Everything below is well-cited and implementable in a few hundred lines of numpy. The verdicts are about your scale — one library, a handful of players, a corpus you could hold in RAM twice over — not about the merits of the papers.

Hu, Koren & Volinsky, 2008Collaborative Filtering for Implicit Feedback Datasets

Take  The canonical treatment of feedback that isn’t a star rating. Its move — separate preference (did they like it) from confidence (how sure are we) — is precisely the anonymous/surveyed distinction, already solved. Alternating least squares over your vote table is seconds of compute.

Axelsson, Nilsson & Berglund, 2010A principal components model of soundscape perception · codified as ISO 12913-2

Take  The two anchored axes. This is what makes your profiles legible to other researchers instead of a private invention — the point the field notes already make. Your iso12913_to_sam note argues SAM is faster to collect; that’s true, and it maps cleanly onto the same two axes, so collect whichever instrument you prefer and store the projection.

Salakhutdinov & Mnih, 2008Bayesian Probabilistic Matrix Factorization

Take  The justification for storing a covariance next to the mean. You do not need the full Gibbs sampler — a diagonal-covariance online Bayesian linear regression per listener gives you the same posterior story at the cost of one vector multiply per vote.

Li, Chu, Langford & Schapire, 2010A Contextual-Bandit Approach to Personalized News Article Recommendation

Take  Day one has no data, and the current _predict_for_player reaches for random.choice to cope. Thompson sampling is the principled version and it is free once you have the covariance: draw u ~ N(μ, Σ), take the argmax. Wide posterior explores, tight posterior exploits, no tuning knob.

Masthoff, 2011Group Recommendation: aggregation strategies

Take  The room problem, surveyed. Its finding — that least-misery and fairness beat averaging when people share a space they can’t leave — validates the layer design. One layer per active listener is the fairness strategy, realised in the audio rather than in the ranking.

Carbonell & Goldstein, 1998 · Kulesza & Taskar, 2012Maximal Marginal Relevance · Determinantal Point Processes

Later  This is the named answer to your open question about scoring a combination. A DPP scores a set by relevance and internal diversity at once, which is exactly “each layer is wanted, and together they don’t mush.” MMR is the two-line greedy version — ship that, and only reach for the determinant if the mixes actually sound crowded.

Rendle et al., 2009BPR: Bayesian Personalized Ranking

Later  Pairwise ranking loss, usually stronger than pointwise on implicit data. Worth an evaluation bake-off against iALS once you have a few thousand votes, but it needs negative sampling machinery you don’t have yet.

Chang et al., 2020Bundle Recommendation with Graph Convolutional Networks

Skip  The formally correct model of “a vote on a mix, not a track.” It also wants item counts in the tens of thousands and a GPU. The gain-weighted mean in Fig. 8 captures most of it; revisit only if layer-level credit assignment demonstrably fails.

PANNs / VGGish / CLAPpretrained audio taggers & embeddings

Later  The obvious filling for the classify.py stub, and it solves item cold-start without waiting for votes. But it is a heavyweight dependency in a container that currently has none, and an artist placing their own recording on the circumplex at upload is better data and one afternoon of work. Do that first; add the model when the catalogue outgrows hand-placement.

Feasibility

What this costs you in Django

Less than you’d think, because the seams are already cut. COSOUND_CORE_PREDICTOR is a documented settings hook resolved through import_string, with the intended value sitting commented out at the bottom of settings.py. Nothing below requires Celery, Redis, a vector database or a training pipeline. It is one migration, one task module, and a predictor swap.

The write path

A vote already has everything the update needs. Keep it off the request: you spent the whole project buying back milliseconds, so the view enqueues and returns.

app/taste.py — sketch 
# One Bayesian ridge step. Diagonal covariance, so it's elementwise.
@task
def fold_vote(vote_id: int) -> None:
    vote = Vote.objects.select_related("voter").get(pk=vote_id)
    m    = mix_vector(vote.cosound)      # gain-weighted mean of layers
    u, p = vote.voter.taste, vote.voter.precision
    y    = 1.0 if vote.value == Vote.UPVOTE else -1.0

    p_new = p + m * m                    # certainty only ever grows
    u_new = (p * u + m * y) / p_new      # evidence pulls the mean

    Listener.objects.filter(pk=vote.voter_id).update(
        taste=u_new, precision=p_new, n_obs=F("n_obs") + 1
    )

Anchored dimensions get a high starting precision for surveyed listeners and a low one for everyone else. That single number is the entire mechanism by which a survey outranks a tap without either signal needing a special code path.

The read path

Replace the tag-mode heuristic in _predict_for_player and point COSOUND_CORE_PREDICTOR at the new one. The shape stays identical — still one layer per active listener, still returning a Prediction for player.update() — so the player, the API and the vote carousel need no changes at all.

The nightly refit — iALS across the whole vote table to re-seat both sides of the space — is one more @task on the same db_worker that already sends your login emails. At a few thousand votes it is a couple of seconds of numpy. This is the part worth saying plainly: your scale is small enough that the honest engineering answer is a numpy function in a database-backed task queue, and reaching for anything heavier would be the mistake.

Three things in the way

Found while reading

None of these are modelling problems, and all three will bite the profile work if they aren’t cleared first.

The 400 ms gap is a scheduling problem, not an algorithm problem

Fig. 4 blames the soundscape’s gradualness, but the delay is in the plumbing. refresh sleeps REFRESH_INTERVAL_SECONDS = 30 between passes, and /api/cosound is throttled to 10/m — a 6-second poll ceiling. Worst case from tap to audible layer is roughly 36 seconds, and no predictor will fix that. Enqueue the prediction from submit_vote on the vote itself, and keep the 30-second sweep as a decay floor. The receipt the field notes want depends on this more than on anything in this memo.

There is no path from an anonymous account to a real one

login_anonymously mints a fresh User; check_email mints a different one. Nothing grafts the first onto the second, so a listener who taps for three weeks and then signs up silently discards every vote they cast — the exact population whose profile is most worth keeping. In posterior terms the merge is free: the anonymous posterior becomes the named account’s prior. In code it doesn’t exist yet. This is the single highest-value gap on the list.

Classification runs at the wrong moment

Sound.save() calls classifier(self.pk) when embeddings is None — but on a first save the pk is still None, and the file hasn’t been committed to storage. The random stub doesn’t care, so it passes today and will fail the moment a real classifier wants to open the audio. Move it to a post-save @task before you replace the stub, not after.

Build order

What blocks what

These are numbered because they genuinely depend on one another, not for tidiness. Steps 1–3 are the unblock; everything after is improvement you can sequence freely.

Fix the anonymous-to-named graft

Before any profile is worth accumulating, it has to survive sign-up. Small, self-contained, and independent of every modelling decision below.

Settle d and add the listener vector

One migration: taste, precision, n_obs, surveyed_at, plus the two ISO floats on Sound. Painful to change later, trivial now.

Put the circumplex in the upload form

SoundForm already carries file, title, artist, set, art, flavor, tags. Two more fields, and item cold-start is solved by the people who made the recordings — no audio model required.

Fold votes into the posterior

The fold_vote task, enqueued from submit_vote. From here every listener, anonymous or not, has a real profile that improves with use.

Swap the predictor and enqueue on vote

Point COSOUND_CORE_PREDICTOR at the vector version, and fire prediction from the vote rather than waiting for the sweep. This is where the tap starts sounding like it did something.

Add the survey at sign-up

Rating the mix already in the air, per the field notes. It supervises both sides of the space at once, and it’s what keeps the anchored axes honest for everyone else.

Nightly iALS refit, then MMR on the stack

Re-seat the free dimensions from the full vote history; add marginal-relevance scoring only once mixes start sounding crowded.

Still open

What this doesn’t settle

  • The instrument. Your SAM note prefers three fast pictographic scales; ISO’s eight items are what the comparison literature is built on. Either works with the anchored design, because both reduce to the same two coordinates — but pick one before collecting, since a mid-study switch invalidates the anchor.
  • Whether gains carry preference. The mix vector treats gain as a mixing weight. It may also be a signal in its own right — someone pulling a layer down is telling you something the vote value doesn’t.
  • The 60-second vote throttle. VOTE_THROTTLE_SECONDS caps a listener at one observation a minute. Fine for abuse control, but it sets a hard ceiling on how fast a profile can converge in a single sitting — worth revisiting once posteriors exist to watch.
  • Vote.section is recorded and unused. Where someone sat is already on every row. Nothing in this memo reads it, and a per-spot model of the room is the obvious next thing it unlocks.

The short version

The roadblock as written — two voter types, one representation — dissolves once you notice both are already the same row. What’s actually missing is a listener vector next to the sound vector you already have, a covariance to say how much you trust it, and two dimensions anchored to a standard so the space means something outside this project. The rest is scheduling.
